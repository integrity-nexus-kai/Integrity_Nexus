# Methodology of Structured Discovery

**Status:** Working foundation methodology  
**Repository authority:** Integrity Nexus  
**Author:** Kai Stefan Dietrich  
**Date:** 2026-08-06

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines a candidate methodology for structured discovery across the TIG-E research ecosystem.

It describes the epistemic function of the principal research stages and establishes rules that prevent one stage from being used as a substitute for another.

It does not claim that every human discovery follows one universal psychological sequence. Its generality, reproducibility, and cross-domain applicability remain open research questions.

---

## 2. Core Epistemic Sequence

```text
Phenomenon / Reality
        ↓
Observation, Measurement, and Perception
        ↓
State Reconstruction
        ↓
Problem and Target Analysis
        ↓
Bottleneck Detection
        ↓
Constraint Extraction
        ↓
Search-Space Orientation and Compression
        ↓
Intuitive Structural Signal
        ↓
Open Structural Hypothesis
        ↓
Evidence Search and Formal Reconstruction
        ↓
Emergence Candidate
        ↓
Crystallization
        ↓
Mathematical / Scientific Validation and Audit
        ↓
Canonicalization Threshold
        ↓
Governance Decision
        ↓
Canonical Knowledge and Memory
```

Reality is not treated as direct epistemic possession. Scientific access begins through observation, measurement, perception, records, and controlled representations.

---

## 3. Epistemic Non-Substitution Rule

> **Research stages may interact recursively, but their epistemic functions may not be exchanged, collapsed, or used as substitutes for one another.**

Core prohibitions:

```text
Intuition ≠ validation
Emergence ≠ proof
Crystallization ≠ derivation
Governance ≠ scientific truth
Canonicalization ≠ evidence
Repository status ≠ epistemic completion
Formal expression ≠ physical interpretation
Compatibility ≠ derivation
```

The reverse direction also requires protection:

```text
Mathematics ≠ automatic search-space orientation
Proof ≠ automatic research-question generation
Formal correctness ≠ structural novelty
Evidence accumulation ≠ automatic theory architecture
Governance completeness ≠ scientific completeness
```

Each stage has a distinct function and authority boundary.

## 4. Function of the Principal Stages

### 4.1 Observation and Perception

Provide the initial accessible material: measurements, experiences, records, anomalies, texts, data, and events.

They do not by themselves determine the correct interpretation.

### 4.2 State Reconstruction

Reconstructs what is already known, claimed, stored, unresolved, or authoritative before new work begins.

It reduces false novelty and duplicate construction.

### 4.3 Problem and Target Analysis

Defines what is wrong, missing, unknown, or inconsistent and what result conditions would count as progress.

A target is not a preferred answer.

### 4.4 Bottleneck Detection

Identifies the unresolved structure most likely to release the greatest amount of admissible progress.

It prioritizes work; it does not solve the bottleneck by naming it.

### 4.5 Constraint Extraction

Makes definitions, invariants, dependencies, evidence requirements, scope limits, and non-circularity conditions explicit.

Constraints organize admissibility; they do not automatically select one surviving candidate.

### 4.6 Search-Space Orientation and Compression

Determines where search should occur and removes, marks, parks, or deprioritizes inadmissible or irrelevant paths.

Compression is not proof.

### 4.7 Intuitive Structural Signal

Provides pre-formal orientation or candidate integration based on implicit pattern recognition, experience, salience, and constrained synthesis.

Intuition may initiate or prioritize reconstruction. It may not authorize a claim.

### 4.8 Open Structural Hypothesis

Converts an implicit signal into an explicit, auditable, revisable research object.

Registration is not confirmation.

### 4.9 Evidence Search and Formal Reconstruction

Seek independent support, counterevidence, alternative explanations, mathematical form, and testable relations.

Formal reconstruction makes a candidate inspectable. It does not make it correct.

### 4.10 Emergence Candidate

Marks the appearance of a previously undocumented higher-order structure from an already organized and constrained system.

Emergence identifies novelty at a structural level. It does not validate the structure.

### 4.11 Crystallization

Tests whether the emerging structure remains stable under continued constraint pressure, comparison, reconstruction, and review.

Persistence is not truth.

### 4.12 Validation and Audit

Evaluate whether the structure satisfies the evidence, derivation, mathematical, empirical, reproducibility, status, and governance obligations corresponding to its claim.

### 4.13 Canonicalization Threshold

Tests whether the current state is sufficiently explicit, bounded, traceable, reviewed, and authorized for canonical preservation.

An unresolved result may cross this threshold if its unresolved status is represented accurately.

### 4.14 Governance Decision

Authorizes placement, status, transition, revision, or rejection within the repository system.

Governance controls representation and process. It does not manufacture scientific truth.

### 4.15 Canonical Knowledge and Memory

Preserve the currently authorized research state together with its provenance, boundaries, dependencies, and open questions.

Canonical knowledge is an authorized representation, not a guarantee of finality.

## 5. Ordered but Recursive Structure

The methodology contains a dependency order, but it is not a rigid one-pass pipeline.

Permitted feedback loops include:

```text
Evidence Search
→ revised Problem Analysis

Formal Reconstruction
→ newly visible Constraints

Constraint Testing
→ renewed Search-Space Compression

Validation
→ revised Open Structural Hypothesis

Crystallization
→ renewed Bottleneck Detection

Canonical Documentation
→ new Open Questions
```

A feedback loop may return to an earlier stage. It may not silently bypass the authority of a later gate.

---

## 6. Retrospective Obviousness

A successfully developed structure may appear obvious in retrospect even though it was not predictable before the relevant constraints, relations, and candidate architecture became visible.

This property is called:

> **Retrospective obviousness**

It may occur when a new structure compresses many previously separate observations into a coherent relation.

Retrospective obviousness is not proof that the structure was necessary, uniquely derivable, or correct.

It is a candidate indicator of successful integration and explanatory compression that still requires validation.

---

## 7. Search-Space Creation and Formal Work

The methodology distinguishes two reciprocal limitations:

1. Pre-formal discovery mechanisms do not replace formal validation.
2. Formal methods do not automatically create the relevant research question, bottleneck, or candidate architecture.

Therefore, structured discovery requires interaction between:

```text
orientation
+ abstraction
+ candidate formation
+ evidence
+ formalization
+ validation
+ governance
```

No single component is granted universal priority across all stages.

---

## 8. The Research Process as Research Object

The documented process may itself become an object of investigation.

Questions include:

- Which stages are reproducible across domains?
- Which transitions are human-specific, AI-assisted, or hybrid?
- Which signals predict successful candidate formation?
- Which feedback loops improve quality and which create fixation?
- How does externalized cognition alter speed, error rates, and continuity?
- Can bottleneck selection and canonicalization readiness be independently audited?

The process described here is an explicit candidate model of structured discovery.

Its generality and effectiveness are not assumed; they require controlled comparison and validation.

---

## 9. Human–AI Division of Function

Humans may contribute:

- embodied and long-duration experience;
- intuition and salience recognition;
- target and value selection;
- abstraction across lived contexts;
- responsibility and authorization.

AI systems may contribute:

- large-context comparison;
- state reconstruction support;
- dependency mapping;
- contradiction detection;
- formal restatement;
- candidate comparison;
- audit preparation.

Neither human conviction nor AI confidence is validation.

The evaluated object is the reconstructed candidate and its support record.

---

## 10. Signal-to-Noise Interpretation

Each stage modifies the research signal-to-noise relation differently:

- observation supplies raw material;
- state reconstruction removes false novelty;
- bottleneck detection selects high-impact signal;
- constraints mark inadmissible routes;
- compression reduces active noise;
- intuition detects weak signal;
- abstraction isolates structure;
- emergence integrates signal;
- crystallization tests persistence;
- validation tests support;
- governance prevents status noise;
- canonical memory preserves bounded signal.

The stages are complementary because each removes a different failure mode.

---

## 11. Failure Modes

Structured discovery fails when:

- the target is defined after a preferred answer has already been selected;
- intuition is protected from falsification;
- compression deletes counterevidence;
- emergence is declared without a constrained precursor system;
- crystallization is confused with proof;
- governance status is presented as scientific validation;
- formal correctness hides an irrelevant or misplaced problem;
- canonicalization erases open questions;
- or the research history becomes impossible to reconstruct.

## 13. Governing Statement

> Preserve the order of epistemic authority even when the path of discovery is recursive: intuition may orient, emergence may reveal, crystallization may stabilize, validation may test, governance may authorize, and canonical memory may preserve—but none may impersonate the function of another.
