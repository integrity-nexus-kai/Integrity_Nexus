# Discovery Engine Architecture

## Locked Mode Scope

This document defines the component architecture of TIG-E as a domain-neutral structured discovery engine.

It does not define a domain theory, prove any result, select any ontology, or promote any candidate into canonical status.

---

## 1. Purpose

TIG-E structures and governs research work in complex, partially observable, high-noise search spaces.

Physics is the first reference domain. It is not identical with the engine itself.

The engine supports the optimization of research signal relative to redundancy, ambiguity, status noise, unresolved dependencies, misplaced content, cognitive overload, and uncontrolled complexity.

---

## 2. Core Mission

```text
TIG-E automates and supports the governance of discovery processes.
It does not automate final scientific authority.
```

The engine may:

- reconstruct system state;
- structure flexible inputs;
- detect bottleneck candidates;
- support abstraction;
- extract constraints and dependencies;
- compress active search spaces;
- preserve intuition signals;
- manage open structural hypotheses;
- support evidence search and formal reconstruction;
- detect emergence candidates;
- support crystallization and validation workflows;
- prepare canonicalization decisions;
- and preserve canonical and controlled non-canonical memory.

## 4. Input and State Retrieval Layer

TIG-E accepts flexible input:

- free text;
- research questions;
- hypotheses;
- mathematical proposals;
- observations and measurements;
- code commits;
- repository files;
- GitHub issues;
- paper fragments;
- audit records;
- intuition events;
- or domain-specific candidate submissions.

Before creating a new object, the engine should retrieve relevant existing assets, registries, decisions, and authority sources.

```text
New input does not imply new object.
Missing retrieval does not imply missing knowledge.
```

---

## 5. State Reconstruction Layer

The State Reconstruction Layer identifies:

- existing assets;
- current canonical and non-canonical states;
- competing versions;
- repository authority;
- known dependencies;
- open questions;
- prior decisions;
- rejected or parked routes;
- and unresolved conflicts.

Its output is a current-state model sufficient for controlled continuation.

State reconstruction is not validation.

---

## 6. Problem and Target Structuring Layer

This layer extracts:

- active problem;
- target state;
- success criteria;
- non-goals;
- scope boundaries;
- claim classes;
- and required evidence or validation.

The engine must distinguish a target condition from a preferred answer.

---

## 7. Bottleneck Detection Layer

This layer identifies and compares candidate bottlenecks.

It evaluates:

- blocked dependencies;
- expected downstream release;
- search-space impact;
- recurrence of failure;
- uncertainty reduction;
- and competing bottleneck candidates.

It proposes an active bottleneck for review.

```text
Detected bottleneck ≠ proven bottleneck.
Priority proposal ≠ final decision.
```

## 8. Structuring and Abstraction Layer

The engine extracts:

- observations;
- claims;
- assumptions;
- candidates;
- definitions;
- dependencies;
- open questions;
- evidence requirements;
- generator/projection relations;
- domain assignments;
- and review risks.

It may also support:

- structural extraction;
- scale transition;
- domain-transfer mapping;
- and principle-candidate formation.

```text
Structured ≠ accepted.
Abstracted ≠ universal.
Transferred ≠ validated.
```

## 9. Constraint, Integrity, Dependency, and Domain Filters

The filters apply:

- candidate admission rules;
- generator/projection testing;
- integrity checks;
- circularity checks;
- dependency-order checks;
- epistemic-status checks;
- terminology checks;
- domain-boundary checks;
- and authority checks.

Filters may retain, route, revise, park, or reject candidate paths.

They do not establish final scientific truth.

---

## 10. Search-Space Compression Layer

The compression layer reduces or clarifies the active search space by removing, marking, parking, or deprioritizing:

- invalid paths;
- circular paths;
- redundant paths;
- premature paths;
- misplaced domain content;
- unresolved projections presented as foundations;
- and candidates that fail active constraints.

Each successful iteration should:

- reduce the active search space;
- clarify its structure;
- expose a dependency;
- improve candidate differentiation;
- or route a necessary decision.

Compression must preserve traceability and counterevidence.

Canonical source:

```text
foundation/03_search_space_compression.md
```

## 11. Intuition and Candidate Capture Layer

The engine may detect or receive pre-formal structural signals.

It supports two roles:

```text
Intuition as Sensor
→ possible bottleneck, contradiction, or neglected relation

Intuition as Synthesis
→ possible integration inside a compressed search space
```

The system may create an Intuition Event record and request reconstruction.

It must not use human conviction or AI confidence as evidence.

Detailed operational source in TIG-E:

```text
docs/research/intuition/TIG-E_Intuition_Operationalization_Framework_v0.1.md
```

## 12. Open Structural Hypothesis Registry

A surviving intuition signal or structural observation may become an explicit Open Structural Hypothesis.

The registry stores:

- identity;
- scope;
- originating signal;
- dependencies;
- constraints;
- expected compression or explanatory value;
- evidence requirements;
- counterevidence;
- and current status.

Registration is not confirmation.

---

## 13. Evidence Search and Formal Reconstruction Layer

This layer supports:

- source retrieval;
- literature and prior-art search;
- evidence classification;
- counterexample search;
- alternative-candidate comparison;
- mathematical formalization;
- dependency restatement;
- computational test preparation;
- and explicit falsification criteria.

Formal reconstruction makes a candidate reviewable. It does not establish correctness.

---

## 14. Constraint and Dependency Testing Layer

Candidate structures are tested against:

- known definitions;
- mathematical consistency;
- evidence boundaries;
- dependency order;
- non-circularity;
- domain standards;
- repository authority;
- and competing explanations.

Possible outputs:

```text
RETAIN
REVISE
REJECT
PARK
SPLIT
ESCALATE
```

---

## 15. Emergence Detection Layer

The engine may identify a previously undocumented higher-order structure arising from an organized and constrained candidate system.

Emergence detection requires traceable precursors and must preserve the difference between:

- originating elements;
- interaction structure;
- resulting higher-order candidate;
- and later interpretation.

```text
Emergence produces a candidate, not proof.
```

Canonical source:

```text
foundation/04_emergence_protocol.md
```

---

## 16. Crystallization Support Layer

This layer records whether a candidate remains stable under:

- continued constraint pressure;
- reconstruction;
- comparison;
- terminology normalization;
- additional evidence;
- and review.

It must distinguish:

- conceptual crystallization;
- governance crystallization;
- and applied crystallization checkpoints.

Crystallization does not itself establish scientific truth.

---

## 17. Validation and Audit Queue

The engine prepares structured review packages for:

- mathematical validation;
- scientific validation;
- evidence audit;
- dependency audit;
- semantic consistency audit;
- governance audit;
- reproducibility review;
- and cross-repository consistency review.

The reviewer may:

- accept;
- reject;
- revise;
- defer;
- split;
- reclassify;
- request evidence;
- request formalization;
- or escalate.

---

## 18. Canonicalization Readiness Gate

Before canonical documentation, the system checks whether the object is sufficiently:

- identified;
- scoped;
- traceable;
- status-clear;
- dependency-complete or accurately open;
- reviewed;
- reconstructable;
- and correctly placed.

Passing this gate means readiness for an authorization decision, not truth or proof.

Canonical governance source:

```text
governance/canonicalization_threshold.md
```

---

## 19. Human Governance Decision

Human authorization determines whether the candidate state is:

```text
CANONICALIZED
RETURNED FOR RECONSTRUCTION
RETURNED FOR AUDIT
REVISED IN STATUS
PARKED
ARCHIVED
REJECTED
```

The engine may prepare the decision. It may not assign final scientific or canonical authority autonomously.

---

## 20. Canonical Memory and Repository Integration

Repository integration may create or update:

- canonical documents;
- controlled non-canonical records;
- registry entries;
- dependency maps;
- audit findings;
- decision records;
- supersession notices;
- change logs;
- and reusable research assets.

Canonical Memory preserves the currently authorized state with its boundaries and open questions.

Controlled non-canonical memory preserves observations, rejected candidates, parked routes, and historical states when they retain research value.

## 21. Signal-to-Noise Objective

The component architecture is coordinated by the working objective:

> Increase relevant, admissible, traceable, differentiating, and generative research structure relative to redundancy, ambiguity, contradiction, status noise, dependency noise, and cognitive overload.

This objective is governed by:

```text
foundation/08_research_signal_to_noise_optimization.md
```

It is a methodological architecture principle, not a validated quantitative metric.

---

## 22. Domain Packs

Domain Packs provide domain-specific:

- ontologies;
- candidate types;
- evidence standards;
- terminology;
- mathematical rules;
- registries;
- validation procedures;
- and review criteria.

Examples include physics, medicine, pharma, psychology, materials science, AI alignment, and software architecture.

```text
Domain Pack ≠ Foundation Layer.
Reference application ≠ universal engine.
```
