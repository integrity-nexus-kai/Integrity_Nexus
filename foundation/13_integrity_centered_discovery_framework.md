## 1. Purpose and Status

This document defines a domain-neutral working framework for relating integrity to the principal mechanisms of structured discovery across the TIG-E research ecosystem.

It synthesizes already documented mechanisms without replacing their detailed source documents.

It does not define a physical integrity field, prove a mathematical invariant, establish a universal cognitive law, or claim that the framework is already validated across domains.

Its purpose is to answer the following architectural question:

> What must remain preserved while observations, representations, candidates, models, evidence states, and repository objects are transformed during structured discovery?

The answer proposed here is **integrity**, understood as a cross-cutting preservation principle.

---

## 2. Semantic Clarification of Integrity

The term integrity is used in many different contexts and must not be collapsed into one undifferentiated meaning.

Integrity is not limited to:

- moral integrity;
- logical consistency;
- mathematical invariance;
- physical stability;
- governance compliance;
- repository cleanliness;
- correctness of language;
- absence of contradiction;
- or successful validation.

These may be local manifestations or tests of integrity, but none alone defines the complete architecture.

The Foundation Layer therefore uses a methodologically restricted working meaning.

---

## 3. Working Definition

> **Integrity is the controlled preservation of identity-defining structure, relevant boundaries, dependencies, provenance, admissibility conditions, and epistemic status across representation, transformation, evaluation, and preservation.**

This definition is domain-neutral and methodological.

It does not imply that the same formal object, metric, or invariant exists in every scientific domain.

---

## 4. Integrity as a Cross-Cutting Preservation Principle

Integrity is not one additional linear stage in the research lifecycle.

It is a preservation requirement acting across all stages.

```text
Observation
→ must preserve source and measurement context

Representation
→ must preserve the architecture represented

Abstraction
→ must preserve relevant structure while removing incidental detail

Domain Transfer
→ must preserve the transferred relation without asserting false identity

Validation
→ must preserve claim boundaries and unresolved dependencies

Canonicalization
→ must preserve the authorized state and its non-claims

Reuse
→ must preserve provenance, scope, and epistemic status
```

The term **integrity invariant** may be used only as a candidate architectural expression unless a formally defined invariant and transformation class are supplied.

Preferred current formulation:

```text
Integrity
= cross-cutting preservation principle
= candidate invariant architecture
≠ established universal mathematical invariant
```

## 6. Integrity Classes

The framework distinguishes multiple integrity classes.

### 6.1 Structural Integrity

Preservation of identity-defining relations, invariants, boundaries, and admissible transformations of an object or system.

### 6.2 Representational Integrity

Preservation of the actual architecture, status, authorship, dependencies, and scope of the object represented.

A representation may be grammatically correct and logically coherent while still lacking representational integrity.

### 6.3 Epistemic Integrity

Preservation of distinctions among:

- observation;
- assumption;
- candidate;
- hypothesis;
- formal construction;
- derivation;
- proof;
- validation;
- compatibility;
- interpretation;
- and open question.

### 6.4 Dependency Integrity

Preservation of the direction, order, unresolved state, and authority of dependencies.

### 6.5 Process Integrity

Preservation of the distinct epistemic function of each research stage.

```text
Intuition ≠ validation
Emergence ≠ proof
Crystallization ≠ derivation
Canonicalization ≠ evidence
Governance ≠ scientific truth
```

### 6.6 Governance Integrity

Preservation of authorized, traceable, non-circular, and scope-correct state transitions.

### 6.7 Canonical Integrity

Preservation of the currently authorized research state together with its provenance, boundaries, dependencies, audit state, and explicit non-claims.

### 6.8 Cognitive Integrity

Preservation of context, continuity, object identity, and unresolved state across externalized cognition, human–AI interaction, and later reconstruction.

### 6.9 Domain-Specific Integrity

Locally defined integrity conditions belonging to a scientific or technical domain.

Examples may include mathematical invariants, physical admissibility conditions, biological viability constraints, software safety properties, or institutional control obligations.

Domain-specific integrity may not be promoted into a universal Foundation primitive without explicit abstraction and review.

Integrity is a preservation condition and admissibility boundary.

```text
Signal-to-Noise Optimization
= increase relevant, traceable, differentiating, generative structure

Integrity
= prevent optimization from destroying identity, provenance,
  admissible alternatives, dependencies, or epistemic boundaries
```

The governing relationship is:

> **TIG-E optimizes the research signal-to-noise relation subject to integrity-preservation constraints.**

Therefore:

```text
Search-space compression
without provenance loss

Noise reduction
without deletion of unresolved evidence

Speed
without claim escalation

Abstraction
without destruction of relevant differences
```

Signal and noise remain scope-relative. Integrity prevents current operational irrelevance from being mistaken for permanent worthlessness.

## 9. Intuition as Experience-Dependent Orientation

Intuition is treated as an experience-dependent capacity for detecting or integrating potentially relevant structural signals before complete formal reconstruction is available.

Its quality may depend on:

- duration and relevance of experience;
- repeated exposure to consequential feedback;
- pattern differentiation;
- abstraction capacity;
- sensitivity to inconsistency;
- familiarity with failure modes;
- calibration against later validation;
- and the degree of search-space compression already achieved.

Intuition has two principal roles:

```text
Intuition as Sensor
→ early low-authority indication of a bottleneck, mismatch, or neglected relation

Intuition as Synthesis
→ candidate integration inside a sufficiently constrained search space
```

Subjective certainty, intensity, experience, or biographical importance are not evidence.

## 10. Bottleneck Detection as a Core Integrity Function

Bottleneck Detection identifies the unresolved object whose clarification is expected to release the greatest admissible progress.

Its integrity function is to prevent effort from being dispersed across visible but non-dominant problems.

```text
Current State
→ Bottleneck Candidates
→ Dependency and Impact Analysis
→ Active Bottleneck
→ Focused Work
→ Changed State
→ Bottleneck Re-Detection
```

A bottleneck is scope-relative and may be:

- epistemic;
- structural;
- representational;
- dependency-related;
- governance-related;
- cognitive;
- implementation-related;
- or environmental.

## 11. Search-Space Orientation Versus Validation

Search-space orientation determines where disciplined work should begin.

Validation determines whether a developed object satisfies the obligations of its claimed status.

```text
Orientation
→ selects or prioritizes a route

Validation
→ tests support, correctness, and limits
```

Neither may substitute for the other.

```text
A promising route is not a validated result.
A validated local result does not automatically identify the correct larger search space.
```

Integrity requires the relationship between these functions to remain explicit.

## 13. Intuition, Emergence, and Crystallization

These mechanisms must remain distinct.

### Intuition

Detects or integrates a potentially relevant structural signal.

### Emergence

Marks the appearance of a previously undocumented higher-order structure from an already organized and constrained system.

### Crystallization

Tests and preserves persistence under further constraint pressure, comparison, reconstruction, and review.

```text
Intuition
→ may orient or propose

Emergence
→ may reveal a higher-order candidate

Crystallization
→ may stabilize and preserve

Validation
→ must test support and correctness
```

The sequence may contain feedback loops, but no stage may impersonate another.

## 15. Cognitive Background Load

### Working Definition

> **Cognitive Background Load is the continuing consumption of attention, working-memory capacity, monitoring effort, or interference tolerance by unresolved, insufficiently externalized, ambiguously classified, or repeatedly reconstructed objects.**

This is a methodological working model, not a clinical or neuroscientific diagnosis.

Candidate mechanism:

```text
Unresolved or weakly externalized object
        ↓
Continued background activation
        ↓
Repeated monitoring or reconstruction demand
        ↓
Reduced free search capacity
        ↓
Higher interference and weaker signal discrimination
```

Possible sources include:

- unresolved tasks;
- duplicate or conflicting documents;
- uncertain canonical authority;
- missing provenance;
- unclosed dependency questions;
- unrecorded decisions;
- ambiguous status;
- physical or digital disorder relevant to active work;
- and repeated fear of losing prior results.

Candidate indicators may include:

- repeated return to the same object without progress;
- inability to release a completed reasoning chain;
- frequent context reconstruction;
- increased error rate under dense parallel work;
- difficulty distinguishing active from parked tasks;
- and reduced capacity for new structural synthesis.

A causal generalization requires independent study.

```text
Canonicalization Threshold ≠ proof threshold
Canonicalization Threshold ≠ publication acceptance

Canonicalization Threshold ≠ finality
```

An unresolved object may cross the threshold when its unresolved state is represented accurately and reconstructably.

## 17. Canonical Asset Preservation

Canonical Asset Preservation protects the current authorized research state against:

- context loss;
- duplicate reconstruction;
- silent status drift;
- dependency loss;
- provenance loss;
- accidental overwrite;
- false novelty;
- and detachment from governing evidence boundaries.

A preserved asset should retain:

- object identity;
- source and authorship;
- current path;

- epistemic status;
- dependencies;
- unresolved questions;
- non-claims;
- and permitted future transitions.

```text
Storage ≠ preservation
```

## 18. Integrity Friction

### Working Definition

> **Integrity Friction is the recurring cognitive, operational, representational, or structural resistance produced when actual working conditions, representations, decisions, dependencies, or environments remain misaligned with the identity-defining principles and preservation requirements of the active system.**

Candidate classes include:

### 18.1 Representational Friction

The representation repeatedly fails to match the architecture represented.

### 18.2 Epistemic Friction

The same object is repeatedly treated as having incompatible epistemic statuses.

### 18.3 Dependency Friction

Work proceeds while required predecessor relations remain unclear, hidden, or violated.

### 18.4 Governance Friction

Authorization, status, placement, or transition rules conflict with the actual research state.

### 18.5 Cognitive Continuity Friction

The system repeatedly loses context and must reconstruct work already performed.

### 18.6 Environmental Friction

The physical or digital working environment generates persistent interference with attention, continuity, retrieval, or orderly task progression.

Integrity Friction is not automatically evidence that an external environment is objectively wrong. It is a candidate indicator of misalignment requiring scope-specific analysis.

## 19. Environment Congruence

Environment congruence describes the degree to which working conditions support the preservation requirements of the active research process.

Relevant factors may include:

- reliable access to canonical assets;
- physical and digital order;
- low unnecessary interruption;
- clear task boundaries;
- stable retrieval paths;
- limited unresolved background objects;
- and compatibility with the operator's established working principles.

Possible relation:

```text
Higher environment congruence
→ lower integrity friction
→ lower cognitive background load
→ improved signal discrimination
→ increased capacity for abstraction and emergence
```

This is a working causal hypothesis. Its generality and magnitude remain unvalidated.

## 20. Human–AI Complementarity

The framework assigns different candidate strengths and responsibilities to humans and AI systems.

### Human contributions may include

- embodied and long-duration experience;
- salience and bottleneck recognition;
- intuition;
- cross-context abstraction;
- target and value selection;
- responsibility;
- and final authorization.

### AI contributions may include

- state reconstruction support;
- large-context comparison;
- dependency mapping;
- contradiction detection;
- formal restatement;
- classification support;
- audit preparation;
- and repeatable document generation.

Integrity requires that the origin and authority of each contribution remain explicit.

```text
Human conviction ≠ validation
AI confidence ≠ validation
AI assistance ≠ authorship
Automation ≠ scientific authority
```

The evaluated object is the reconstructed candidate and its support record.

## 21. Operational Integrity Questions

Before or after a material transformation, the system should ask:

1. What object is being transformed?
2. What gives the object its identity?
3. Which relations, boundaries, or invariants must be preserved?
4. Which changes are admissible?
5. Which dependencies must remain visible?
6. Which provenance must remain attached?
7. What is the current epistemic status?
8. Does the transformation alter that status?
9. Which information is being treated as noise?
10. Could a weak but relevant signal be destroyed?
11. Is the result reconstructable without the originating conversation?
12. Has abstraction removed a relevant distinction?
13. Has domain transfer been confused with identity or proof?
14. Has emergence been confused with validation?
15. Has crystallization been confused with derivation?
16. Has governance been assigned scientific authority it does not possess?
17. Is the object ready for the Canonicalization Threshold?
18. Which Integrity Friction remains?
19. Which Cognitive Background Load remains?
20. What must be preserved for the next research cycle?
