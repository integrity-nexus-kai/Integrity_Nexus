# Externalized Cognition and Canonical Memory

**Status:** Working foundation architecture  
**Repository authority:** Integrity Nexus  
**Author:** Kai Stefan Dietrich  
**Date:** 2026-08-06

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines Externalized Cognition and Canonical Memory as cognitive-infrastructure mechanisms within the TIG-E research architecture.

It describes how research states, structural signals, decisions, dependencies, and open questions may be externalized into governed artifacts so that further work does not depend exclusively on transient human memory, one conversation, one agent, or one active session.

It does not claim that a repository is itself a conscious or autonomous cognitive system.

---

## 2. Working Definitions

### Externalized Cognition

> **Externalized Cognition is the governed transfer of relevant internal or conversational research structure into explicit, inspectable, reusable external artifacts that participate in subsequent reasoning and coordination.**

### Canonical Memory

> **Canonical Memory is the controlled preservation of the currently authorized research state together with its provenance, epistemic status, scope, dependencies, unresolved questions, and non-claims.**

Externalization makes structure available. Canonicalization determines which representation is currently authoritative.

## 3. Core Cognitive Cycle

```text
Human Intuition or Observation
        ↓
Structural Signal
        ↓
Human–AI Assisted Explication
        ↓
Governed Externalization
        ↓
Review and Status Classification
        ↓
Canonical or Controlled Non-Canonical Memory
        ↓
Cognitive Load Release
        ↓
Renewed Search Capacity
        ↓
Further Bottleneck Detection, Intuition, and Emergence
        ↺
```

The cycle is recursive. Preserved structure changes the conditions of the next search.

---

## 4. Repository as Cognitive Infrastructure

Within this architecture, a repository is more than a passive archive.

It may function as:

- an external working-memory support;
- a state-reconstruction source;
- a dependency and authority map;
- a preserved record of rejected, parked, and retained candidates;
- a coordination layer between human and AI agents;
- a mechanism for preventing duplicate reconstruction;
- a source of reusable validated or accurately bounded structures;
- and an institutional memory independent of the originating conversation.

This functional role does not grant the repository scientific authority beyond its governed contents.

---

## 5. Cognitive Load Release

Externalization can reduce cognitive load by removing the need to keep all of the following active simultaneously:

- prior decisions;
- exact terminology;
- cross-repository dependencies;
- historical alternatives;
- open-question status;
- evidence boundaries;
- file paths and authority relations;
- unfinished work packages;
- and the reasons for earlier rejection or deferral.

The released capacity may support renewed comparison, abstraction, bottleneck detection, and candidate formation.

Cognitive relief is not evidence that the preserved content is correct.

---

## 6. Canonical and Non-Canonical Memory

The system should preserve more than canonical results.

### Canonical Memory

Contains the currently authorized representation of a research state.

### Controlled Non-Canonical Memory

May contain:

- observations;
- intuition events;
- open structural hypotheses;
- rejected candidates with reasons;
- parked paths;
- audit findings;
- historical snapshots;
- superseded formulations;
- and unresolved alternatives.

```text
Not canonical
≠ disposable

Preserved
≠ validated
```

Non-canonical memory supports provenance, comparison, and future reinterpretation.

---

## 7. Minimum Memory Properties

A reusable externalized research object should preserve:

- identity;
- title and path;
- originating context;
- author or responsible actor;
- date and version;
- scope;
- epistemic status;
- governance status;
- dependencies;
- evidence or support references;
- open questions;
- non-claims;
- supersession relations;
- and authorized next actions.

Without these properties, externalization may preserve text while losing research meaning.

---

## 8. Signal-to-Noise Function

Externalized Cognition improves the research signal-to-noise relation when it:

- prevents repeated rediscovery of existing assets;
- reduces memory-driven inconsistency;
- makes conflicts and dependencies visible;
- preserves weak signals until later reconstruction;
- separates active work from archived context;
- prevents unmarked status escalation;
- and enables precise retrieval of the currently relevant state.

It creates noise when it:

- produces uncontrolled document proliferation;
- preserves duplicate or conflicting authority without mapping it;
- stores content without status metadata;
- treats every intermediate thought as canonical;
- or makes retrieval harder than reconstruction.

---

## 9. Asset Preservation Rule

> **Research value must not remain dependent on the continued availability of the originating chat, memory state, person, or AI session.**

An asset should be externalized when its loss would materially impair:

- state reconstruction;
- provenance;
- dependency understanding;
- reproducibility;
- auditability;
- or continuation of the research program.

Externalization should occur at the smallest level that preserves the necessary structure without generating unnecessary duplication.

---

## 10. Externalization Threshold

Not every transient statement requires repository preservation.

A candidate object should be externalized when one or more of the following applies:

- it changes the architecture or active research state;
- it identifies a bottleneck or dependency;
- it introduces a reusable distinction;
- it records an important rejection or decision;
- it would be difficult to reconstruct accurately;
- it affects multiple repositories or work packages;
- it defines a new governed process object;
- or it is likely to be reused in later validation, product design, or audit.

Externalization does not imply canonicalization.

---

## 11. Human–AI Complementarity

### Human role

Humans may contribute:

- salience and target selection;
- embodied and long-duration experience;
- intuition and abstraction;
- responsibility for approval;
- and recognition of when an insight has strategic value beyond the current task.

### AI role

AI systems may contribute:

- structured explication;
- file and dependency mapping;
- comparison against existing assets;
- status normalization;
- retrieval and restatement;
- consistency checking;
- and preparation of canonicalization records.

Neither actor should rely on memory alone when the repository can preserve the governed state.

## 12. Memory Lifecycle

```text
Signal or Research Object
        ↓
Preservation Decision
        ↓
Asset Discovery and Duplicate Check
        ↓
Classification and Placement
        ↓
Externalization
        ↓
Review
        ↓
Canonical / Controlled Non-Canonical / Archive
        ↓
Retrieval and Reuse
        ↓
Revision or Supersession
```

A later revision must preserve the relationship to the earlier state.

---

## 13. Failure Modes

The architecture fails when:

- the repository becomes an undifferentiated transcript archive;
- document quantity substitutes for clarity;
- conflicting versions lack authority markers;
- externalization occurs before asset discovery;
- a summary erases important uncertainty or non-claims;
- the active state can only be reconstructed by reading the full history;
- canonical memory contains unsupported status promotions;
- or old assets are deleted merely because their current relevance is low.

---

## 14. Evaluation Candidates

Future evaluation may examine:

- time required for state reconstruction;
- duplicate-asset rate;
- frequency of status inconsistency;
- ability of independent agents to continue work;
- retrieval precision;
- cognitive reconstruction burden;
- number of preserved weak signals later producing useful candidates;
- and rate of canonical memory correction after audit.

These metrics require operational definition and calibration.
