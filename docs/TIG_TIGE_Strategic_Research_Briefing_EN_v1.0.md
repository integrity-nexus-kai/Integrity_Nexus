## 1. Executive Summary

### What is TIG?

TIG - Topological Integrity Gravity - is the physical research program. It investigates whether spacetime is fundamental or emerges as an effective structure from deeper topological integrity conditions. "Topological" refers to fundamental relations such as connectedness, neighborhood, boundaries, and orientation before distances and curvature are assumed; however, these relations do not automatically determine a unique physical metric. "Integrity" is not used in a moral sense, but as the mathematically describable structural self-consistency of admissible states and transitions.

### What scientific objective does TIG pursue?

The objective is a mathematically consistent and physically testable framework in which gravitational and quantum-mechanical structures can be investigated as distinct effective manifestations of a common, deeper ordering and update structure. Dynamics, geometry, gravitation, quantized transitions, and robust field equations are intended to be derived from and validated within that structure. This objective defines a research program, not an already completed demonstration.

### What is TIG-E?

TIG-E - the Topological Integrity Gravity Engine - is not merely an AI-assisted analytical tool, but an integrated human-AI research infrastructure. It combines search-space compression, versioned documentation, Open Question and blocker registries, adversarial review, and governance against terminological and theoretical drift into a controllable research process. TIG is the first large-scale scientific application and validation case for this infrastructure.

### What has already been established?

Publicly documented work includes a validated field-equation architecture within the current TIG realization, a canonical spherical realization, the exact cubic horizon structure x³ - x² + β³ = 0, critical horizon transitions, the GR, Schwarzschild, and Newtonian limits, and representative geometries with bounded curvature. Open issues include, among others, the fundamental derivation of the integrity tensor, a variational principle, covariant and dynamical closure, the microscopic update structure, the possible reconstruction of a metric from pre-metric relations, and independent review.

## Decision-Maker Perspective

### 2. Why This Research Program Could Matter

Its potential value lies on two connected levels: TIG opens a new, testable research path in fundamental physics, while TIG-E is intended to accelerate the underlying process of discovery in highly complex search spaces. For a strategic reader, the relevant question is therefore not only whether the physical hypothesis is promising, but also whether the underlying infrastructure can substantially reduce research time, cost, and avoidable trial-and-error.

#### 2.1 Scientific Value

The reconciliation of general relativity and quantum physics is among the most difficult open problems in fundamental research. TIG asks a logically prior question: Which topological relations, states, and transitions are admissible, stable, and realizable within an overall physical structure - and can effective spacetime emerge from those conditions?

#### 2.2 From Undirected Trial-and-Error to Structured Search

In many fields of research, very large sets of possible hypotheses, candidates, or experimental paths must be examined. Experiments and failed attempts remain indispensable. TIG-E is intended, however, to reduce the undirected, redundant, and poorly documented portion of that search:

- Search spaces are progressively compressed and decomposed into testable subproblems.

- Contradictory or structurally inadmissible paths should become visible earlier.

- Negative results and rejected paths remain available as usable knowledge.

- Expensive laboratory work, computing resources, and expert effort can be concentrated on better-founded candidates.

#### 2.3 Potential Leverage in Time and Resources

The framework is intended to improve more than communication or task management. It is designed to reduce the epistemic cost of search: less context loss, less duplicated effort, fewer silent shifts in meaning, earlier recognition of dead ends, and faster concentration on viable structures. The magnitude of this effect must be measured against defined comparison cases; no fixed percentage gains are therefore claimed at present.

#### What is the potential value for the recipient or their network?

Early access to a potentially relevant physical research approach and to a research infrastructure that - following successful validation - could also shorten research cycles in medicine, biotechnology, materials research, energy, AI safety, and other complex domains.

## Physical Core

### 3. The Core Scientific Problem and the Direction of TIG

General relativity and quantum physics are each extraordinarily successful, but they have not yet been unified in a fully consistent fundamental description. TIG investigates whether part of this gap arises because spacetime, geometry, and state admissibility are ordinarily assumed in advance, rather than making their conditions of emergence part of the theory itself.

#### 3.1 Why "Topological"?

Topology describes fundamental relations within a system - connectedness, neighborhood, boundaries, orientation, and admissible connections - initially without relying on concrete distances, angles, or an already assumed metric. By itself, topology determines neither physical scales nor a unique metric. TIG therefore asks first which structural relations must be preserved, changed, or taken through a critical transition before an effective spacetime geometry can be described or reconstructed.

#### 3.2 What Does "Integrity" Mean?

Within the TIG framework, integrity denotes the capacity of a system to preserve the joint consistency of its constitutive relations, boundary conditions, and admissible transitions. It is not a moral property, but a potentially formalizable property of a system. Topology describes how structures are connected; integrity determines which of those relations and transformations are admissible and mutually consistent.

#### 3.3 Concrete Scientific Objective

TIG investigates whether spacetime is not fundamental, but an emergent macroscopic structure. The broader target hypothesis is that gravitational geometry and quantum-mechanically discrete state or energy transitions could arise from a common, deeper integrity and update structure. A viable theory would have to derive dynamics, geometry, gravitation, quantization, and suitable field equations from that structure, while also reproducing established physical limits.

#### 3.4 Conceptual Development Chain

##### Working Architecture

Topological relations → structural integrity → admissible states → realization / update → quantized transitions and dynamics → open reconstruction of causal and metric structure → effective geometry, spacetime, and gravitation

This chain describes a direction of research, not an already complete derivation. In particular, the realization and update dynamics remain an open interface between state admissibility, quantized transitions, and effective geometry.

#### 3.5 The Metric as an Open Bridge Question

The current TIG field-equation architecture is formulated metrically and already assumes $g_{\mu\nu}$; it does not yet derive the metric from a pre-metric topological-integrity structure. The extended research program investigates whether, and under which additional conditions, order, neighborhood, and transition relations can first yield a causal or conformal structure and ultimately a complete Lorentzian geometry.

#### 3.6 Possible Microscopic Cellular Structure

A current research hypothesis examines whether a four-dimensional cellular or hypercubic fundamental structure - represented mathematically, for example, by hypercells or tesseracts - could provide a microscopic carrier for the update dynamics. Three-dimensional sections could display cubic cells; transitions across boundaries could provide a structural mechanism for discrete transfer of energy or action and represent torsion as an orientational, transport, or closure defect.

##### Status of This Hypothesis

A discrete cellular structure does not automatically explain quantization or torsion. State spaces, couplings, update rules, conservation conditions, the continuum limit, and Lorentz behavior must first be formalized and tested. The hypothesis must therefore be clearly distinguished from the TIG results already published.

#### 3.7 Relation to Established Physics

TIG does not claim to replace established physics with a conceptual narrative. A viable formulation must reproduce known limiting cases, be mathematically well defined, and be assessed against predictions that are testable in principle. Derived results, validated architecture, hypotheses, and open research programs are therefore identified separately.

## Methodological Innovation

### 4. TIG-E: Integrated Human-AI Research Infrastructure

TIG-E is not merely a single AI tool, nor is it a project-management system. It is an integrated human-AI research infrastructure. It combines search-space compression, versioned documentation, open questions, adversarial review, and governance against terminological and theoretical drift into a controllable research process. TIG constitutes the first large-scale scientific application and validation case for this infrastructure.

#### 4.1 Compression of Highly Complex Search Spaces

Large research problems are decomposed into defined subspaces until concrete, testable research assignments emerge. Dependencies remain visible; assumptions, derivations, results, hypotheses, Open Questions, blockers, and external objections receive separate status positions.

#### 4.2 Operationalization of Emergence

Decomposition, comparison, recombination, adversarial checking, and renewed structuring form an iterative cycle in which new relationships can become visible more systematically. The objective is not to produce emergence arbitrarily, but to make the conditions of emergent knowledge more methodically describable, reproducible, and controllable over the long term. Complete formalization remains a research task in its own right.

#### 4.3 Governance Against Research and Terminological Drift

In long-running or AI-infrastructure-supported projects, terms, assumptions, and objectives can change unnoticed. Versioning, status labels, Open Question and blocker registries, documented corrections, adversarial reviews, external feedback, and consistency audits are intended to make this drift visible, containable, and correctable.

#### 4.4 Human, AI Infrastructure, and Scientific Expertise

The AI infrastructure supports search-space decomposition, comparison, recombination, adversarial checking, documentation, and consistency control. It replaces neither scientific responsibility nor independent review. The system architect defines search spaces, governance, and integration logic; subject-matter scientists examine the mathematical, physical, and empirical substance. Initial expert feedback from academic environments is already being incorporated selectively; the next phase is intended to broaden this exchange systematically.

#### 4.5 Generalizability

TIG is the first large-scale physical application and validation case for TIG-E. The mechanism is designed in principle for cross-domain application, for example in materials research, medicine, biotechnology, energy systems, or AI safety. Every transfer, however, requires its own subject-matter expertise, data, adaptations, and independent validation.

##### Methodological Core

Complexity is not explained away. It is organized through structured decomposition, controlled recombination, documented emergence, and governance, so that knowledge can arise more rapidly and remain stable over time.

## State of the Work

### 5. Current Scientific Status and Validation Roadmap

The public TIG layer comprises a field-equation architecture, a canonical spherical realization, the exact cubic bifurcation structure of horizon formation, and a conditional analysis of vacuum admissibility and bounded curvature. The architecture is currently formulated metrically. The status "validated field-equation architecture" signifies neither a derivation of the metric from pre-metric relations nor the existence of a fully derived, dynamically closed, and empirically confirmed fundamental field theory.

#### 5.1 Existing Field-Equation Architecture

$G_{\mu\nu} = I_{\mu\nu}[g, r_c]$

In the current public realization, the Einstein tensor $G_{\mu\nu}$ is set equal to an integrity tensor $I_{\mu\nu}$ that depends on the metric $g$ and an integrity scale $r_c$. This architecture has been structurally validated within the documented model. The independent definition and fundamental derivation of the integrity tensor, the variational formulation, the covariant extension, and dynamical closure remain open in particular.

A central next review step is the variational foundation of the field-equation architecture. It must be investigated whether a suitable action functional or Lagrangian density exists from whose variation the TIG structure can be derived consistently. Such a derivation would be essential for the independent definition of the integrity tensor, the identification of the dynamical degrees of freedom, and the examination of covariant closure. It is not yet available and belongs to the highest-priority open research tasks.

#### 5.2 Available Result: Horizon Equation

x³ - x² + β³ = 0

The equation describes the exact cubic bifurcation structure of horizon formation within the current spherical TIG realization. At the critical point, a double root occurs at x = 2/3; it follows that β³ = 4/27. A more detailed classification is provided in the technical appendix.

## Technical Appendix

**Concepts, field-equation architecture, horizon structure, research hypotheses, and governance**

### A. Separation of Concepts and Levels

#### A.1 TIG and TIG-E

- TIG denotes the physical research program, its theoretical architecture, results, hypotheses, and Open Questions.

- TIG-E denotes the integrated human-AI research infrastructure, the discovery mechanism, and the governance framework.

- TIG is the first large-scale physical application and validation case for TIG-E.

#### A.2 Topology and Integrity

- Topology: connectedness, neighborhood, boundaries, orientation, and admissible connections prior to, or independently of, a concrete metric; these relations do not automatically determine a physical metric.

- Integrity: consistent preservation or controlled change of constitutive relations under admissible transitions.

- Topological integrity: a working term for the preservation or controlled change of fundamental structural relations under admissible state transitions.

#### A.3 Metric and Pre-Metric Structure

The existing TIG field-equation architecture is formulated metrically and uses $g_{\mu\nu}$ as its geometric variable. It is therefore not a fundamental derivation of the metric from a pre-metric topology.

It remains open whether additional order, causal, measure, or volume structures can first determine a conformal geometry and subsequently a complete Lorentzian geometry. Existence, uniqueness, dimension, signature, the continuum limit, and the connection to the existing field-equation architecture must be examined separately.

#### A.4 Status Terms

**Validated architecture:** A documented structure has been consistently examined within the stated assumptions; this does not automatically constitute a complete fundamental theory.

**Derived result:** A result follows, under clearly identified assumptions, from a documented derivation.

**Research hypothesis:** A reasoned possibility that has not yet been fully derived or empirically confirmed.

**Active program:** A defined development and review path with open work steps.

**Open question:** A dependency, definition, or derivation has not yet been closed.

### B. Field-Equation Architecture and Horizon Structure

#### B.1 Current Field-Equation Architecture

$$G_{\mu\nu} = I_{\mu\nu}[g, r_c]$$

$G_{\mu\nu}$ denotes the Einstein tensor, $I_{\mu\nu}$ the current realization of the integrity tensor, $g$ the metric, and $r_c$ the integrity scale. The public TIG layer classifies this structure as a validated field-equation architecture or structurally validated research candidate. A determination of the tensor independent of the particular realization, a variational principle, full covariance, and dynamical closure remain open.

A central next review step is the variational foundation. It must be investigated whether an action functional or Lagrangian density exists from whose variation the TIG structure follows consistently. This would be essential for determining the tensor, identifying the dynamical degrees of freedom and conservation properties, and establishing covariant closure; no such derivation is currently available.

#### B.2 Metric Status and Open Reconstruction

The current realization assumes a metric spacetime. Topological relations and structural admissibility alone guarantee neither distances, time intervals, Lorentzian signature, light cones, scale, volume element, connection, nor a unique metric. A future reconstruction program must therefore explicitly determine the pre-metric fundamental objects, causal order, dimension, signature, local scale, existence and uniqueness of the metric, and the continuum limit.

#### B.3 Spherical Realization

m(r) = M r³ / (r³ + r_c³)

F(r) = 1 - 2 M r² / (r³ + r_c³)

This realization leads to the dimensionless horizon condition. It is a concrete model realization and must not be equated with the complete fundamental theory.

#### B.4 Exact Horizon Equation

x³ - x² + β³ = 0

Critical transition: x_c = 2/3, β_c³ = 4/27, β_c = (4/27)^(1/3)

Here x = r_H/(2M) and β = r_c/(2M). The discriminant of the cubic equation changes sign at the critical value. The transition separates regions with different real-root and horizon structures and thus describes a precise bifurcation structure of horizon formation within the current realization.

#### B.5 Boundaries of the Claim

- The horizon equation is not equivalent to a complete covariant field theory.

- By itself, it explains neither the microscopic emergence of spacetime, the reconstruction of a metric, nor the quantization of physical transitions.

- Its physical significance must be examined within the complete TIG architecture and against established physics.

### C. Possible Microscopic Integrity Structure

#### C.1 Working Hypothesis: Hypercells

It is being investigated whether a four-dimensional cellular or hypercubic structure could provide a deeper carrier of physical states. A four-dimensional hypercell is commonly called a tesseract; three-dimensional sections or projections can display cubic cells. The decisive elements, however, would not be the visually intuitive cubes themselves, but the state spaces, neighborhoods, boundaries, and admissible update rules.

#### C.2 Quantized Transitions

Discrete transitions between cells could provide a structural mechanism for quantized transfer of energy or action. A discrete lattice alone, however, does not prove physical quantization. A defined state space, coupling and update rules, conservation conditions, and a derivation of the elementary unit of action or energy are required.

#### C.3 Torsion

In a cellular architecture, torsion could be represented as an orientational, transport, or closure defect at boundaries. The cellular structure does not make torsion fundamentally possible for the first time; it could, however, provide a microscopic discrete representation. The relation to spin, the continuum limit, and the effective geometric description must be investigated in particular.

#### C.4 Required Validation

- a mathematically consistent cellular complex and unambiguous update dynamics;

- derivation of quantized, rather than merely numerically discrete, transitions;

- an appropriate continuum limit and recovery of established GR limits;

- Lorentz symmetry, or a precise explanation of its effective emergence;

- a connection to the field-equation architecture, the horizon equation, and an explicit metric-reconstruction path;

- a clear distinction from external hypotheses concerning quantization of action.
